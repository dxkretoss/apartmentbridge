About this community

Cedar Grove Commons is a 238-unit tax-credit community built in 2019, two blocks from the Truxel Road bus rapid transit line. Apartments include full kitchens, in-unit washer hookups, and central air. The gated courtyard has a playground, community garden beds, and a resident services office offering free after-school tutoring and job coaching. Rents are set by income tier and never exceed 30% of the tier's limit.

Programs, explained

Government housing help,
without the jargon

Five main programs put a roof over millions of households. Here's how each one works, who qualifies, and how to apply — in plain words.

Friendly illustration style: flat geometric buildings in brand blues & greens

Section 8 · Housing Choice Voucher

Most flexible

You get a voucher from your local housing authority and rent any private apartment whose landlord accepts it. You pay roughly 30% of your income; the voucher pays the rest, up to a local payment standard.

Income: usually ≤ 50% AMIApply: housing authorityWaitlists common

LIHTC · Tax-Credit Apartments

Most homes

Privately run apartment communities built with tax credits in exchange for capped rents. If your household earns under the building's limit (usually 60% of area median income), you can apply directly — no voucher needed.

Income: ≤ 50–80% AMI by buildingApply: at the propertyFixed below-market rent

HOME Program

Federal money that cities and states turn into local affordable rentals, rehab grants, and down-payment help. Rules vary by city, which is why every HOME listing on Doorway links to its local requirements.

Income: ≤ 60–80% AMIApply: city/state agencyRentals + ownership help

Section 202 · Senior Housing

62+

Affordable apartments reserved for very-low-income seniors, usually with supportive services like meals, transportation, and on-site coordinators. Rent is about 30% of income.

Age 62+ · income ≤ 50% AMIApply: at the property

FHA Loans

For buying

Not a rental program — an insured mortgage that lets first-time buyers purchase with as little as 3.5% down and flexible credit requirements. A common next step after renting affordable.

3.5% down · 580+ creditApply: FHA-approved lender

Eligibility basics

How income limits work

Every program compares your gross household income to your county's Area Median Income (AMI), adjusted for household size. The bands below are the ones you'll see on listings:

≤ 30% AMI"Extremely low income" — priority for vouchers & public housing
≤ 50% AMI"Very low income" — vouchers, Section 202, many LIHTC units
≤ 60% AMIThe most common LIHTC apartment limit
≤ 80% AMI"Low income" — HOME rentals & workforce housing
